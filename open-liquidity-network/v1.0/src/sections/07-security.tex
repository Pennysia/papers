\section{Security Model}\label{sec:security}

Security depends on venue controls, enforcement during execution, and protection of the user's signing keys.

\subsection{Venue protections}

Venue-specific protections, such as solver bonds or contract controls, continue to apply. Pennysia validates execution independently, while retaining exposure to the underlying venue's implementation and settlement model.

\subsection{Enforced execution}

Execution checks enforce the authorized terms and verify the resulting transfer.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Order validation.} Routes and quotes are checked against the signed payload before execution. Public-chain transaction reordering~\cite{flashboys} requires validation of execution conditions.
  \item \textbf{Spending authority.} Atomic routes use a single Settlement approval, with transfers constrained by the authorized input and recipient.
  \item \textbf{Fill verification.} Settlement records require a match to the signed terms, including the output minimum and deadline. Atomic fallback follows the procedure in Section~\ref{sec:rails}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Account and key security}

Table~\ref{tab:authority} separates account operations from signing authority. A compromised Pennysia session must not grant the server the ability to sign or move assets; wallet authorization remains the user's control over transfers.

\subsection{Residual risks}\label{sec:risk}

The design retains the following dependencies and exposures.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Latency and filler availability.} Cross-chain settlement depends on filler liveness. Market movements or unavailable fillers can cause expiry and require a new quote. Multiple filler networks and alternative routes reduce this dependence where available.
  \item \textbf{Venue counterparty exposure.} Deposit-address settlement depends on the venue delivering output or honoring a refund. The quote discloses the settlement window and counterparty model.
  \item \textbf{Approval concentration.} A unified approval concentrates contract risk in Settlement, making its implementation and verification critical.
  \item \textbf{Wallet-provider dependence.} Embedded wallets rely on co-secured key material and provider-assisted recovery~\cite{threshold}. External wallets support users who prefer a different key-management model.
\end{itemize}

New contracts are intended to follow the prototype's verification process (Appendix~\ref{app:v1}), with open source, invariant testing, and formal analysis where tractable. Deployment artifacts and audit results accompany each chain rollout. Common execution checks simplify integration but do not remove venue-specific risks.
