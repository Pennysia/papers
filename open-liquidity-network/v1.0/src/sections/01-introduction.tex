\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Crypto liquidity is fragmented across chains and venues with different access requirements~\cite{sok-defi}. The price available to a trader depends on where their assets are held and which providers they can access. Reaching a better quote elsewhere can require moving funds or opening another account, adding costs that reduce its advantage.

The \textbf{chain boundary} separates markets that maintain their own liquidity and settlement infrastructure. Execution quality varies by pair and order size, so a better quote may sit on a different network from the user's funds. Accessing it can involve bridge fees and settlement delays, as well as bridge risk~\cite{bridges}. The user must account for these costs and arrange execution across both networks before a price comparison becomes actionable.

The \textbf{access boundary} separates permissionless protocols from venues that require accounts or verification. These markets expose different liquidity and operate under different custody and settlement terms. Depth varies with market conditions and venue design~\cite{dexdepth}, so either group may offer a better price for a given order. A trader seeking access to both must navigate their separate onboarding processes and trading interfaces.

For the user, this fragmentation compounds the practical work of self-custody. Direct control over assets~\cite{bitcoin} comes with responsibility for key management and transaction authorization, while gas and cross-chain transfers require additional coordination. Centralized exchanges consolidate much of this work within an account by holding assets on the user's behalf. Pennysia aims to provide comparable ease of access and execution speed while preserving user control over funds. Figure~\ref{fig:journey} illustrates the workflows the network seeks to simplify.

Intent-based execution~\cite{intentauction} lets users authorize an outcome while an executor submits the transactions and pays gas. Embedded wallets~\cite{smartaccounts} make user-controlled addresses accessible through familiar login methods. Together, these mechanisms support a common interface to a growing onchain market (Figure~\ref{fig:dexshare}).

Pennysia v1, the Open Liquidity Network (OLN), extends the Ethereum prototype (Appendix~\ref{app:v1}) across chain families and liquidity services. Its scope includes spot trading, lending, perpetuals, prediction markets, and vaults supplied by external providers. Pennysia provides the account, routing, and settlement coordination through which users access them, and a common integration through which providers compete for eligible orders.
